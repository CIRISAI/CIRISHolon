# DEVICEGEN-1 — results, 2026-11-12. Branch (a): the generator ships with its cells. Drawing the connection, the computer and the speaker together earns its place: a fair rival that draws each kind alone reaches a clustering of 1.38 where the survey has 2.85.

*Prereg `DEVICEGEN1_PREREG.md`, the record before the parse
`devicegen1/BEFORE_THE_PARSE.md`, generator `engine/crates/holon-scene/src/devices.rs`,
fitting and scoring `devicegen1/devicegen1.py`, log `devicegen1/run1.log`, numbers
`devicegen1/results.json`, tables and sidecar under `tables/devicegen1/`. Fitted on 9,948
households in 26 states; scored on 8,548 in the other 25, each under 20 seeds. One run.
Every household scored was generated by the shipped function.*

## The stake (S7): MET

Does drawing three things together earn anything over drawing each alone?

| | clustering of "no computer" with "no fixed line" | share of households with neither |
|---|---|---|
| the survey, states never seen | 2.85 ± 0.15 | 0.087 |
| the shipped generator | 2.92 | 0.088 |
| the rival: each kind alone, given household size, building, any child, any adult over 64 | 1.38 | 0.028 |

- The stake needed the rival under `1.5` and the generator inside the survey's gap. The
  kill was the rival matching the survey.
- So household size, building and who lives there explain only a little of why the
  households with no computer are the ones with no fixed line. A generator without the
  cells would give three times too few households with nothing to host on.

## The chain (S8, a reading): drawing building apart from size costs almost nothing

With sizes from the household generator's US row, and building drawn either given size or
at the overall share: mean counts move by `−1.1 %` (desktops), `−0.4 %` (televisions),
`−0.4 %` (set-top boxes), `−2.0 %` (smart speakers). In the survey 46 % of one-person
households are in apartments and 13 to 23 % of larger ones, but the means barely notice.
The twin may draw the two apart without harm to these counts.

## Composition against the census (S9, a reading)

- After the chain 21.3 % of generated persons are children and 20.9 % are over 64. The
  survey's own records give 20.6 % and 21.6 %.
- The census's national figures, from memory and NOT read: about 22 % under 18 and about
  17 % over 64. If that is right the survey's households hold too many older adults by
  about four points, and so do the generator's. To be checked against the census before
  anything is keyed on age.
- A one-person household has an adult over 64 in 54 % of houses and 35 % of apartments.
  The composition step takes the building for that reason.

## What went wrong on the way

- One plant's first carrier was wrong and was corrected before the read (a random subset
  of apartments is not "explained by building"). The rival and the stake were unchanged.
- One departure, stated before the read: the table's expectation "with no draw" is not
  computed; the 20-seed mean is what stands beside the survey.
- G0's cell bar of `0.002` was read with the check's own Monte Carlo error added; the
  difference observed was `0.0019`.

## What this is not

- Not which devices are on, how many routers, or whether a desktop is left running.
- Not the twin's "home node in 0.60 of households", which still has no reference.
- Not outside the United States, and 2020 was a pandemic year.

## What follows from it

- The twin can call this after the household generator: a household's size in, its people
  and devices out, with the households that have nothing to host on in the right number.
- Smartphones need a rule that bends with household size at both ends, or an anchor from
  a person-level survey.
- A German device check exists only as national means per 100 households.
